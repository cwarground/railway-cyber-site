GPS Spoofing Threatens Rail Safety

Railway Cyber Intel · 20 Jul 2026

GPS Spoofing: A Growing Concern

In recent years, the threat of GPS spoofing attacks has emerged as a significant concern for the railway industry. These malicious attempts to manipulate GPS signals can have devastating consequences for rail safety and operations.

Misaligned Trains: A Real-World Example

The potential impact of GPS spoofing was demonstrated in 2019 when a train in Pennsylvania collided with a maintenance-of-way vehicle due to incorrect positioning data. The crew had been fooled by attackers into believing their train was aligned with the tracks, when it was not.

Standards and Guidance

To address this growing threat, standards bodies like the American Railway Engineering and Maintenance-of-Way Association (AREMA) have issued guidelines on how to secure GPS systems against spoofing. Key recommendations include using redundant positioning sources, implementing monitoring software, and training staff to identify spoofed signals.

Practical Implications

Rail operators must take these threats seriously and implement robust cybersecurity measures to protect their critical infrastructure. By following best practices outlined in standards like AREMA's Manual for Railway Engineering, companies can enhance the resilience of their GPS-dependent systems against evolving cyber threats.
