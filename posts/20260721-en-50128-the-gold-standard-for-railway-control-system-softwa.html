EN 50128: The Gold Standard for Railway Control System Software

Railway Cyber Intel · 21 Jul 2026

Software plays a crucial role in ensuring the safe operation of railway control and protection systems. In this article, we'll explore the EN 50128 standard, which sets the benchmark for software quality and integrity within the rail industry.

Understanding EN 50128

EN 50128 is a European standard that provides guidelines for the development, operation, maintenance, and assurance of software used in safety-related railway systems. It was first published in 1997 by CENELEC (European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardization) and has since been updated to its latest version, EN 50128:2011+A1:2019.

Key Clauses and Requirements

The standard is divided into several clauses that address various aspects of software development and management. Some key requirements include:

  • Clause 6 - Life Cycle Processes: EN 50128 emphasizes the importance of following a rigorous life cycle process for developing safety-related software. This includes activities such as requirement specification, design, implementation, testing, and maintenance.
  • Clause 7 - Software Safety: The standard provides guidelines on how to assess and mitigate risks associated with software used in railway systems. It defines different levels of software integrity and requires developers to demonstrate compliance with safety requirements.
  • Clause 9 - Configuration Management: EN 50128 stresses the need for effective configuration management practices to ensure that changes to the software are properly controlled and documented. This includes version control, traceability, and change authorization procedures.
  • Clause 10 - Quality Assurance: The standard outlines various quality assurance techniques, such as reviews, audits, and inspections, to verify the correctness and completeness of the software development process.

Real-World Implications

To ensure compliance with EN 50128, railway operators must implement a robust software engineering process that adheres to the standard's requirements. This involves training staff, establishing procedures, and regularly auditing the software lifecycle to verify compliance. Additionally, rail companies must maintain detailed records of all software changes and safety assessments, which can be reviewed by external auditors as part of the certification process.

Practical Implication

For rail operators looking to ensure the safety and reliability of their control systems, adopting EN 50128 provides a proven framework. By implementing the standard's guidelines, organizations can significantly reduce software-related risks and maintain the highest standards of integrity in their critical systems.
