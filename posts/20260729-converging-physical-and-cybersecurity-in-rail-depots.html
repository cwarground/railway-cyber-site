Converging Physical and Cybersecurity in Rail Depots

Railway Cyber Intel · 29 Jul 2026

As the railway industry embraces digitalization, the lines between physical and cybersecurity have blurred. Rail depots, once solely concerned with the safety of trains and personnel, now find themselves at the nexus of both worlds. This convergence necessitates a holistic approach to security that encompasses both realms.

Risk Landscape

The risk landscape for rail depots has evolved. Traditional physical threats, such as theft or vandalism, still persist. However, the introduction of advanced technologies like Internet of Things (IoT) devices, industrial control systems (ICS), and operational technology (OT) has opened new avenues for cyber attacks.

Convergence Standards

To address this evolving threat landscape, standards bodies have issued guidelines promoting the convergence of physical and cybersecurity. For example, ISO/IEC 27001:2013 outlines best practices for an Information Security Management System (ISMS), which can be adapted to include OT and physical security. Similarly, NIST SP 800-53 provides controls that span both cyber and physical domains.

Real-world Attacks

The convergence of physical and cybersecurity is not merely theoretical. In 2018, the NotPetya malware attack on Ukraine's railway system caused widespread disruption, highlighting the potential impact of cyber threats on rail infrastructure. Such incidents have prompted operators to re-evaluate their security posture.

Best Practices

To effectively secure rail depots, organizations must adopt a converged approach that integrates both physical and cybersecurity measures. This includes:

  • Implementing access control systems that incorporate both physical key cards and digital authentication methods.
  • Integrating video surveillance with cybersecurity monitoring to detect unauthorized access attempts or suspicious activities.
  • Conducting regular security audits that assess both the physical and cyber components of depot security.
  • Training personnel on the importance of convergence and their roles in maintaining a secure environment.

Practical Implication

Rail operators must prioritize the implementation of converged security measures. By integrating physical and cybersecurity, organizations can create a more resilient and cohesive defense strategy that protects both digital assets and personnel. This approach ensures that vulnerabilities across domains are identified and mitigated, ultimately safeguarding the integrity of rail operations.
