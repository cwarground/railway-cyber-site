Attack Case Study

DDoS Attack Halts Danish Trains

Railway Cyber Intel · 03 Sep 2026
Photo by Markus Winkler on Pexels

Introduction

In September 2022, the Danish state railway (DSB) experienced a devastating distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attack that brought much of its infrastructure to a standstill. The attack, which targeted DSB's IT systems, caused widespread disruption across the country's rail network, forcing the cancellation of numerous trains and stranding thousands of passengers. This attack, one of the most significant to hit the rail industry in recent years, underscores the critical importance of robust cybersecurity measures in protecting critical infrastructure from emerging threats.

Attack Details

According to official reports, the DDoS attack was carried out using a botnet of compromised devices, which launched a coordinated flood of traffic at DSB's IT systems. The attack, which began in the early hours of the morning, quickly overwhelmed the railway's defenses, rendering its ticketing systems, booking platforms, and other critical services inoperable. As the scale of the attack became clear, DSB was forced to take its systems offline, leading to widespread confusion and frustration among passengers.

Impact and Response

The impact of the attack was felt throughout Denmark's rail network, with thousands of passengers left stranded and numerous trains canceled. DSB, working in conjunction with law enforcement and cybersecurity experts, worked tirelessly to restore services, but it took several hours for the railway to begin to return to normal. In the aftermath of the attack, DSB vowed to take steps to bolster its cybersecurity defenses, including investing in advanced DDoS protection and conducting regular security audits. The attack also prompted calls from politicians and industry experts for greater investment in rail cybersecurity across the board.

Practical Implications

For rail operators and security engineers, the DSB attack serves as a stark reminder of the ever-evolving threat landscape and the need to maintain robust cybersecurity defenses. While it is impossible to protect against every potential threat, there are steps that can be taken to mitigate risk and ensure the smooth operation of critical rail infrastructure. These include investing in advanced DDoS protection, conducting regular security audits, and staying informed about the latest threats and vulnerabilities. By prioritizing cybersecurity and taking a proactive approach to risk management, rail operators can help to keep passengers safe and ensure the smooth operation of the network.
