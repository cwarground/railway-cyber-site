Securing Remote Access for Rail Maintenance Vendors

Railway Cyber Intel · 25 Jul 2026

Railway maintenance is increasingly reliant on remote access solutions, allowing vendors to connect to safety-critical systems from untrusted networks. However, these arrangements introduce significant cybersecurity risks that must be mitigated through robust policies and technical controls.

Remote Access Risks

The primary risks associated with remote access include unauthorized access, credential compromise, data exfiltration, and the introduction of malware or other threats into operational technology (OT) environments. To address these risks, rail industry standards such as IEC 62443 and ISO/IEC 27001 provide guidance on implementing effective access control and risk management processes.

Best Practices

Key best practices for securing remote access to railway systems include:

  • Policies and Procedures: Develop comprehensive policies that define the scope, requirements, and procedures for remote access. Ensure these policies align with industry standards like IEC 62443 and ISO/IEC 27001.
  • Principle of Least Privilege: Implement access controls that adhere to the principle of least privilege, granting users only the permissions necessary to perform their job functions.
  • Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Enforce MFA for all remote access sessions to prevent credential compromise and unauthorized access.
  • Monitoring and Auditing: Continuously monitor and audit remote access activities to detect and respond to suspicious behavior or policy violations.

Practical Implication

Rail operators can mitigate risks associated with remote access by implementing these best practices. By developing comprehensive policies, enforcing strong access controls, and maintaining vigilant monitoring, rail operators can ensure the security of their critical systems while enabling necessary maintenance activities to be performed remotely.
