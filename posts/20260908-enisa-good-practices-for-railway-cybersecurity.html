ENISA Good Practices for Railway Cybersecurity

Railway Cyber Intel · 08 Sep 2026
Photo by Jean-Francois Frenel on Pexels

The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) has published a set of good practices aimed at enhancing cybersecurity within the railway sector. These guidelines, which align with the requirements of ISO/IEC 27001, the international standard for information security management systems, offer a comprehensive framework for rail operators to protect their critical infrastructure from cyber threats.

Key Recommendations

  • Establish a cybersecurity management system (CSMS): Implement a formal structure to manage cybersecurity throughout the organization, following the ISO/IEC 27001 standard.
  • Assess and treat risks: Regularly identify and assess potential cybersecurity risks, and develop strategies to mitigate or eliminate them.
  • Provide cybersecurity training: Ensure that all employees, especially those with access to critical systems, receive appropriate cybersecurity awareness training.
  • Conduct cybersecurity audits: Periodically review the effectiveness of your cybersecurity measures and make necessary improvements.

Standards and Frameworks

The ENISA good practices closely align with several established standards and frameworks, such as:

  • NIST Cybersecurity Framework: A set of guidelines and best practices for organizations to manage and reduce cybersecurity risks.
  • ISO/IEC 27001: The international standard for information security management systems, which provides a comprehensive framework for implementing, maintaining, and continually improving a cybersecurity management system.
  • SP 800-53: A security control baseline developed by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) for use by organizations in various sectors, including transportation.

Real-World Implications

Practical Implication

Implementing these cybersecurity good practices can have significant real-world benefits for rail operators. By establishing a robust CSMS, conducting regular risk assessments, and investing in employee training, organizations can significantly reduce their exposure to cyber threats. This can lead to increased operational resilience, reduced downtime, and improved customer confidence in the security of their journeys.
