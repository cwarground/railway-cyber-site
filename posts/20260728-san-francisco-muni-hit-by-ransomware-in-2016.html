San Francisco Muni Hit by Ransomware in 2016

Railway Cyber Intel · 28 Jul 2026

In September 2016, the Municipal Railway system of San Francisco, California, USA was struck by a ransomware cyberattack. Attackers used Locky malware to encrypt files across Muni's IT infrastructure, demanding Bitcoin payments to decrypt data. No evidence exists that the attackers accessed operational technology (OT) systems directly.

Attack Details

The attack began when an employee opened a malicious Microsoft Word document attachment sent via email. This allowed Locky ransomware to spread and encrypt files across 2,500 Muni endpoints. Muni IT staff worked with law enforcement to investigate the incident. It is unclear how much data was lost or whether any payments were made to the attackers.

Cybersecurity Standards for Rail

Rail cybersecurity standards like IEC TS 62443 and ISO/SAE 21434 provide best practices for protecting critical rail infrastructure against cyber threats. Key requirements include:

  • System categorization and risk assessment (TS 2.x, 4.2)
  • Data handling and security policy development (TS 3.x, 5.2)
  • Access control and authentication (TS 4.x)
  • Safety-related system requirements (21434-1, 21434-2)

Rail Cyber Incident Examples

Other notable rail cybersecurity incidents include:

  • 2020: North American passenger rail operator hit by ransomware
  • 2021: European high-speed rail company targeted by sophisticated attack campaign
  • 2019-ongoing: Multiple attacks on signaling systems and infrastructure of various railway operators worldwide

Practical Implication

Rail operators should assess cybersecurity risks to OT/IT systems, implement industry-standard security controls as outlined in IEC TS 62443 and ISO/SAE 21434, regularly test incident response plans, and partner with qualified cyber defense firms to protect against increasingly sophisticated threats.
