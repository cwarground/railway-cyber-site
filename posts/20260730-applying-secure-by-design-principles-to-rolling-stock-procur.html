Applying Secure-by-Design Principles to Rolling Stock Procurement

Railway Cyber Intel · 30 Jul 2026
Photo by Christina Morillo on Pexels

As the rail industry moves towards a more connected future, the procurement of new rolling stock presents an opportunity to integrate secure-by-design principles. This means building cybersecurity measures directly into the hardware and software of new trains, rather than treating security as an afterthought or add-on. Applying such principles is critical in today's threat landscape, where attacks like ransomware, data theft, and even physical disruptions have targeted rail networks worldwide.

Standards and Frameworks

To guide this effort, standards organizations like the International Union of Railways (UIC) and the European Association for Railway Operators and Companies (EVO) have developed guidelines on secure-by-design. Key frameworks include the UIC Leaflet 772-6, which specifies requirements for cybersecurity in rail systems, and EVO's Technical Recommendation 1031 on IT security management. These documents provide a foundation of best practices to be followed during rolling stock procurement.

Concrete Measures

Specific secure-by-design measures can include implementing hardware-enforced security controls like Trusted Platform Modules (TPMs), requiring all software components to have limited and controlled interfaces, and using formal verification methods to ensure the correctness of safety-critical systems. Procurement specifications should also require suppliers to adhere to industry-recognized cybersecurity standards such as ISO/IEC 27001 for information security management.

Practical Implications

For rail operators, adopting a secure-by-design approach during rolling stock procurement means significantly reducing the attack surface and long-term costs associated with retrofitting cybersecurity into legacy systems. It also ensures that new trains are interoperable and compatible with existing IT infrastructure, avoiding the complexity of integrating separate security solutions later on.
