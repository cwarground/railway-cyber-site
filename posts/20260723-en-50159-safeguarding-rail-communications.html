EN 50159: Safeguarding Rail Communications

Railway Cyber Intel · 23 Jul 2026

The rail industry relies heavily on digital communication to keep trains moving safely and efficiently. But as with any interconnected system, potential vulnerabilities exist that could be exploited by malicious actors seeking to disrupt operations or endanger lives.

Standard Overview

EN 50159 is a key European standard that provides guidelines for designing and implementing safety-related communication in transmission systems for railway applications. Published in 2011 by the CENELEC standards organization, it covers aspects such as:

  • Identifying threat scenarios and determining relevant security requirements
  • Specifying security functions to be implemented in the system
  • Assessing the level of risk posed by potential threats
  • Ensuring proper documentation and traceability of decisions made during the process

Real-World Impact

In 2018, a cybersecurity incident involving the manipulation of data transmitted between signaling systems on Germany's high-speed railway network came to light. While no accidents were reported, the incident highlighted the potential consequences of failing to adequately secure critical rail communications.

Best Practices

To address the challenges posed by EN 50159, rail operators and infrastructure managers should consider implementing a comprehensive approach that includes:

  • Educating staff about cybersecurity risks and best practices
  • Conducting regular vulnerability assessments of transmission systems
  • Maintaining up-to-date documentation on security measures taken
  • Leveraging industry frameworks like ISO/IEC 27001 for overall information security management

Practical Implication

Rail operators seeking to enhance the safety and resilience of their operations should prioritize aligning with EN 50159. By doing so, they can reduce the risk of costly incidents arising from insecure communications, while also demonstrating a commitment to the highest standards of safety.
